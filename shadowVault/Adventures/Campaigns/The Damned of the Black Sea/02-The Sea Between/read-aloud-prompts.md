# Read-Aloud Rewrite Prompts — Die See dazwischen

One self-contained prompt per read-aloud (16 total). Each block below can be copy-pasted as-is into another AI model. The prompts are in English; the required output is German prose. Quoted dialogue lines are motifs referenced elsewhere in the adventure and must survive verbatim.

## Prompt 1 — Act 1: Der Tempel stürzt ein

```
You are a professional German prose stylist rewriting boxed read-aloud text for a tabletop RPG adventure.

CONTEXT: "Die See dazwischen" is a German-language Shadowdark RPG adventure — grim, nautical-occult ghost-story horror. The player characters (PCs) have just destroyed an artifact called the Shard of the Deep inside a sunken temple, freeing the Leviathan, an ancient sea god sleeping beneath the island. Their own captain, Rauthorn, will shortly execute them for it on a night beach. They will then wake aboard an impossible little fishing ketch on a grey sea between death and life — but they must never learn this for certain.

HARD RULE: Nothing in any read-aloud may ever confirm that the PCs die or are dead. Every uncanny detail must remain deniable.

SCENE POSITION: This is the very first read-aloud of the adventure, triggered the instant the Shard shatters. The temple begins to collapse; the scene's job is panic and momentum — it pushes the PCs toward a glowing vision-pool that is the only way out. Tone: seismic, overwhelming, the sense of something vast being born below.

FACTS AND IMAGES THAT MUST SURVIVE (all load-bearing):
- The Shard's light does not go out — it bursts/shatters outward.
- A soundless concussion felt in the teeth; the chamber rings like a struck bell that will not be silenced.
- The blue veins in the black stone flare blindingly, pulse racing, then the pulse stumbles, thins, gutters — like a lamp running out of oil (this "failing pulse" is a recurring motif tied to the Leviathan).
- Something immense rolls over far below — deeper than the temple, deeper than the island's roots — and the whole world moves with it. (This is the Leviathan being born; never name it.)
- The dome cracks; seawater jets from joints that were solid stone a heartbeat ago.
- The smell of deep water — cold, old, patient — pressing UP from below, as if the sea has decided to climb the temple and take them.

MUST NOT: name the Leviathan or explain what moves below; mention game mechanics, dice, or the GM; address the players out of character; confirm anything supernatural as fact rather than perception.

STYLE: German, second person plural (ihr/euch), present tense. Written to be read aloud at the table: strong rhythm, concrete sensory detail, no overwrought metaphors, no repeated sentence patterns. Length: about 3 short paragraphs, under 90 seconds spoken.

CURRENT DRAFT (the author is dissatisfied with the prose — treat it as a content outline, not a quality bar; rewrite from the ground up rather than lightly polishing):

Das Licht des Splitters erlischt nicht — es zerbirst. Ein Schlag ohne Klang, den ihr in den Zähnen spürt, und die Kammer dröhnt wie eine geschlagene Glocke, die niemand mehr zum Schweigen bringt. Die Adern im schwarzen Stein flammen auf, blau und blendend, ein rasender Puls — dann stolpert der Puls, wird dünn, verglimmt wie eine Lampe, der das Öl ausgeht.

Irgendwo unter euren Füßen, tiefer als der Tempel, tiefer als die Wurzeln der Insel, wälzt sich etwas Gewaltiges herum. Die ganze Welt bewegt sich mit.

Dann reißt die Kuppel. Salzwasser schießt aus Fugen, die vor einem Herzschlag noch fester Stein waren — und mit dem Wasser kommt der Geruch: tiefes Wasser, kalt und alt und geduldig, das von unten heraufdrängt, als hätte die See beschlossen, den Tempel hinaufzuklettern und euch zu holen.

TASK: Rewrite this read-aloud in German. Preserve every listed fact and image. Return only the finished German text.
```

## Prompt 2 — Act 1: Das Becken öffnet sich

```
You are a professional German prose stylist rewriting boxed read-aloud text for a tabletop RPG adventure.

CONTEXT: "Die See dazwischen" is a German-language Shadowdark RPG adventure — grim, nautical-occult ghost-story horror. HARD RULE: Nothing in any read-aloud may ever confirm that the PCs die or are dead. Every uncanny detail must remain deniable.

SCENE POSITION: Seconds after the collapse begins. The PCs fled and got exactly one room: the corridor behind them has just collapsed. This read-aloud closes the trap and presents the pool as the only exit. Its second job is foreshadowing: the pool shows the beach they are about to surface into, seen from underwater.

FACTS AND IMAGES THAT MUST SURVIVE (all load-bearing):
- The Corridor of Wailing folds in on itself like a swallowing throat; its carved stone faces are still screaming as it eats them.
- Then silence so sudden it rings in the ears; dust rolls across the floor and settles like snow; the round chamber is the last quiet place left in the world.
- The pool no longer shows visions. It glows — soft, deep, bluish, like light seen through meters of water.
- Where the PCs' reflections should be, there is no surface at all. Instead: shallow water over bright sand seen FROM BELOW — a night sky rippling above the surface, warm orange firelight (alive, from somewhere on shore) dancing across it, blurred figures moving up there: legs wading through surf, shadows hurrying along a beach. (This is literally the beach scene they will surface into — keep every element.)
- Closing beat: the pool is a door; it is only waiting for them to understand.

MUST NOT: explain where the door leads or why; mention game mechanics or the GM; break the underwater perspective of the vision.

STYLE: German, second person plural (ihr/euch), present tense. Written to be read aloud: strong rhythm, concrete sensory detail, no purple prose. Length: about 3 short paragraphs, under 90 seconds spoken.

CURRENT DRAFT (the author is dissatisfied with the prose — treat it as a content outline, not a quality bar; rewrite from the ground up rather than lightly polishing):

Vor euch faltet sich der Korridor des Wehklagens in sich zusammen wie eine schluckende Kehle. Die steinernen Gesichter schreien noch, während er sie frisst — dann Stille, so plötzlich, dass sie in den Ohren klingelt. Staub rollt über den Boden und legt sich wie Schnee. Die runde Kammer ist der letzte stille Ort, der in der Welt noch übrig ist.

Das Becken in ihrer Mitte zeigt keine Visionen mehr. Es leuchtet — weich, tief, bläulich, ein Licht wie durch Meter von Wasser gesehen — und wo eure Spiegelbilder sein sollten, ist keine Oberfläche mehr. Da ist flaches Wasser über hellem Sand, von unten gesehen: ein Nachthimmel, der sich über der Oberfläche kräuselt, und Feuerschein — warm, orange, lebendig — der von irgendwo am Ufer darübertanzt. Gestalten bewegen sich dort oben, vom Wasser verwischt: Beine, die durch Brandung waten, Schatten, die einen Strand entlanghasten.

Das Becken ist eine Tür. Es wartet nur darauf, dass ihr es begreift.

TASK: Rewrite this read-aloud in German. Preserve every listed fact and image. Return only the finished German text.

```

Ihr brecht durch die Oberfläche in kaltes Salz und Lärm — mitten in eine Schlacht hinein. Und aus dem schwarzen Wasser waten ihnen Matrosen entgegen, die nie mit euch gesegelt sind — aufgedunsen, von Seepocken gepanzert, triefend, und hinter ihren Augen brennt ein grünes Licht wie Laternen, durch Faden von Wasser gesehen.

Von hier könnt ihr ihn nicht sehen. Aber ihr seht, was er sieht: ein Glühen, das aus dem verborgenen Ort aufsteigt und in den Nachthimmel klettert. Ein Licht, das es gestern noch nicht gab.

## Prompt 4 — Act 1: Das Urteil

```
You are a professional German prose stylist rewriting boxed read-aloud text for a tabletop RPG adventure.

CONTEXT: "Die See dazwischen" is a German-language Shadowdark RPG adventure — grim, nautical-occult ghost-story horror. The PCs destroyed the Shard of the Deep — their captain Rauthorn's "one chance to never die" — freeing the Leviathan, a sea god. On a night beach, mid-battle against drowned things, Rauthorn has just quietly asked them what happened in the temple, and now knows. This read-aloud is his death sentence over them. The unwinnable execution fight follows immediately. The PCs must never learn for certain what their deaths mean.

HARD RULE: Nothing in any read-aloud may ever confirm that the PCs die or are dead.

SCENE POSITION: The emotional peak of the prologue. The horror is quietness and betrayal by family: the crew they bled beside minutes ago turns away from them. Rauthorn does not rage — he uses the voice he saves for traitors.

FACTS, IMAGES AND DIALOGUE THAT MUST SURVIVE (all load-bearing; quoted dialogue VERBATIM — "Die See kennt keine Gnade" is a campaign motif):
- Rauthorn says: „Die See kennt keine Gnade." He does not shout; he uses the quiet voice he keeps for traitors — and that quietness is the worst part.
- His indictment, verbatim or near-verbatim: „Ich gab euch mein Schiff. Meine Karte. Meine eine Chance, niemals zu sterben. Und ihr habt sie dem Wasser verfüttert."
- The blue light in his eyes stops flickering and HOLDS, steady as a lamp behind glass. (Blue light = the Shard's influence; keep it unexplained.)
- The crewmates the PCs just bled beside turn away from the surf, blades still wet; their faces close like doors, one after another; no one meets the PCs' eyes.
- He repeats: „Die See kennt keine Gnade" — and adds „Und ich auch nicht." — and the saber rises.

MUST NOT: add a fight description (the mechanics follow separately); soften or reword the quoted lines; explain the blue light; mention game mechanics or the GM.

STYLE: German, second person plural (ihr/euch), present tense. Written to be read aloud: controlled, quiet menace; short sentences; no melodrama. Length: about 3 short paragraphs, under 60 seconds spoken.

CURRENT DRAFT (the author is dissatisfied with the prose — treat it as a content outline, not a quality bar; rewrite from the ground up rather than lightly polishing):

„Die See kennt keine Gnade“, sagt Rauthorn. Er schreit nicht. Er spricht mit der leisen Stimme, die er für Verräter bewahrt. Gerade diese Ruhe macht es schlimmer. „Ich gab euch mein Schiff. Meine Karte. Meine eine Chance, niemals zu sterben. Und ihr habt sie dem Fischen verfüttert.“

Das blaue Licht in seinen Augen steht plötzlich still. Kein Flackern mehr. Nur dieses kalte, gleichmäßige Leuchten. Die Männer, an deren Seite ihr eben noch geblutet habt, wenden sich euch mit finsterer Miene zu. Unsicherheit und Zwiesplat die ihr in ihren Gesichtern seht weicht plötzlich tödlicher Absicht. 

„Die See kennt keine Gnade", sagt der Kapitän noch einmal, und der Säbel steigt.

TASK: Rewrite this read-aloud in German. Preserve every listed fact and keep all quoted dialogue verbatim. Return only the finished German text.

## Prompt 5 — Act 1: Die Wiedergeburtsvision

```
You are a professional German prose stylist rewriting boxed read-aloud text for a tabletop RPG adventure.

CONTEXT: "Die See dazwischen" is a German-language Shadowdark RPG adventure — grim, nautical-occult ghost-story horror. The PCs destroyed the Shard of the Deep, freeing the Leviathan, an ancient sea god — and were executed for it by their captain on a night beach. This read-aloud is the transition between their deaths and waking aboard the Halcyon, a small fishing ketch on the sea between death and life, ferried by the Leviathan in the disguise of an old sailor. This vision is the ONLY dream of the entire adventure, and it must stay deniable — a drowning dream, an echo of a vision-pool they used before.

HARD RULE: Nothing may ever confirm that the PCs died. The vision must read as possibly-a-dream.

SCENE POSITION: All PCs have fallen. No place — darkness, cold, sinking. The vision shows the Leviathan being born (they once saw this image in the temple's vision-pool) and its acknowledgment of a debt to them; then the wake-up transition: wood, warmth, three creaks. The three creaks in fixed order are the adventure's heartbeat motif and the trigger for Act 2.

FACTS AND IMAGES THAT MUST SURVIVE (all load-bearing):
- Darkness, cold, slow endless sinking; no bottom will ever catch them.
- The image they once saw in a pool of still water is now around them and REAL: a storm-torn black sea, and out of it rises a shape without measure — bigger than ships, bigger than cliffs, water falling from its flanks like collapsing coastlines. Its eyes burn like two cold stars. (The "two cold stars" are the Leviathan's signature across the campaign — keep them.)
- It is not waking. It is being BORN — and the sea itself makes room for it.
- Its gaze finds each of them, individually. Something passes through them that has no words and needs none; the closest words they will ever find: a debt, acknowledged. (Keep this exact idea — "eine Schuld, die anerkannt wird" — it is the seed of the whole adventure.)
- Then something vast glides past in the dark, close enough to touch: an eye longer than a boat, watching them the whole way past. It is not curious. It memorizes them / imprints them on itself.
- Transition out: cold, dark — then wood under the cheek. Warm, dry wood, gently rising and falling. Somewhere above, a rope creaks. Then another creak. Then a third. In order. Always in order. Like a heartbeat.

MUST NOT: name the Leviathan; state that they died or are between worlds; mention game mechanics or the GM.

STYLE: German, second person plural (ihr/euch), present tense. Written to be read aloud slowly: hypnotic rhythm, short paragraphs, concrete images over abstraction. Length: about 5 very short paragraphs, under 2 minutes spoken.

CURRENT DRAFT (the author is dissatisfied with the prose — treat it as a content outline, not a quality bar; rewrite from the ground up rather than lightly polishing):

Dunkelheit. Kälte. Ihr sinkt — langsam, endlos, und da ist kein Grund, der euch je auffangen wird.

Dann ist das Bild, das ihr einmal in einem Becken stillen Wassers gesehen habt, um euch herum und wirklich: eine sturmzerrissene schwarze See, und aus ihr steigt eine Gestalt, für die es keine Maße gibt — größer als Schiffe, größer als Klippen, Wasser stürzt von ihren Flanken wie einbrechende Küstenlinien. Ihre Augen brennen wie zwei kalte Sterne. Sie erwacht nicht. Sie wird geboren — und die See selbst macht ihr Platz.

Ihr Blick findet euch. Jeden Einzelnen von euch. Etwas geht durch euch hindurch, das keine Worte hat und keine braucht, und das Nächste, was ihr je an Worten dafür finden werdet, ist dies: eine Schuld, die anerkannt wird.

Dann gleitet etwas Gewaltiges im Dunkeln an euch vorbei, nah genug, um es zu berühren: ein Auge, länger als ein Boot, das euch die ganze Zeit im Vorbeigleiten ansieht. Es ist nicht neugierig. Es prägt sich euch ein.

Kälte. Dunkel. Und dann — Holz unter eurer Wange. Warmes, trockenes Holz, das sich sacht hebt und senkt. Irgendwo über euch knarrt ein Tau. Dann wieder — ein anderes Knarren. Dann ein drittes. Der Reihe nach. Immer der Reihe nach. Wie ein Herzschlag.

TASK: Rewrite this read-aloud in German. Preserve every listed fact and image. Return only the finished German text.
```

## Prompt 6 — Act 2: Erwachen an Bord der Halcyon

```
You are a professional German prose stylist rewriting boxed read-aloud text for a tabletop RPG adventure.

CONTEXT: "Die See dazwischen" is a German-language Shadowdark RPG adventure — grim, nautical-occult ghost-story horror. The PCs were executed by their captain on a night beach after destroying the Shard of the Deep (which freed the Leviathan, a sea god). They now wake aboard the Halcyon, an impossibly small two-masted fishing ketch on a windless grey sea. The old sailor at the helm is secretly the Leviathan in a failing human disguise, ferrying their souls back toward life; his cover story — island sank, found them adrift, sailing them home — is a plausible lie the players must be able to believe.

HARD RULE: Nothing may ever confirm that the PCs died. Every uncanny detail must remain individually deniable. The old man's story must stay plausible.

SCENE POSITION: The first read-aloud of Act 2, directly continuing the third creak that ended the rebirth vision. It must establish: the ship, the wrong-but-calm sea and sky, the old man, the lantern, and his cover story. Mood: eerie calm, not threat — the players should want to believe him.

FACTS, IMAGES AND DIALOGUE THAT MUST SURVIVE (all load-bearing; the old man's line VERBATIM):
- They wake on warm, dry planks; the first thing the world gives them is a sound: rope creaking — three creaks, always the same three, in the same order. Like a heartbeat someone has taken over for them. (Heartbeat motif — keep it.)
- A patched brown sail above, full and steady — though not a breath of wind stirs.
- The boat is small: two masts, drying nets aft, a stern rudder — a fishing ketch, the kind that fishes a coast in the morning and runs home before the weather in the evening. There is no coast. There is no evening.
- The sea lies flat as poured glass from horizon to horizon, under a grey twilight that contains no sun anywhere.
- At the helm: an old man in a heavy tarred canvas coat, older than any fashion, one big calm hand on the wheel. Beside him a lantern burns warm and low — the only warm color in the world. (The lantern is the adventure's central protective object — keep "the only warm color in the world" or an equally strong version of it.)
- He watches them sit up one by one and nods. Without surprise. As if they were on time.
- His line, verbatim: „Die Insel ist versunken", sagt er. „Hab euch treibend im Wrack gefunden. Ich segle euch nach Hause."

MUST NOT: make the old man sinister (he must read as trustworthy-yet-odd); confirm anything about death; mention game mechanics or the GM.

STYLE: German, second person plural (ihr/euch), present tense. Written to be read aloud: calm, precise, quietly uncanny. Length: about 4 short paragraphs, under 90 seconds spoken.

CURRENT DRAFT (the author is dissatisfied with the prose — treat it as a content outline, not a quality bar; rewrite from the ground up rather than lightly polishing):

Ihr erwacht auf warmen, trockenen Planken, und das Erste, was ihr wahrnehmt, ist ein Geräusch: das Knarren von Tauwerk. Drei Knarrlaute, immer dieselben drei, in derselben Reihenfolge. Wie ein Herzschlag.

Über euch zieht ein geflicktes braunes Segel, prall und stetig — dabei rührt sich kein Wind. Das Boot ist klein. Zwei Masten, achtern trocknende Netze, ein Ruder am Heck — eine Fischerketsch, die Sorte, die morgens eine Küste befischt und abends vor dem Wetter nach Hause läuft. Es gibt keine Küste. Die See liegt glatt wie gegossenes Glas von Horizont zu Horizont, unter einem grauen Zwielicht, das nirgendwo eine Sonne enthält.

Am Ruder steht ein alter Mann in einem schweren, geteerten Mantel, eine große, ruhige Hand am Rad. Neben ihm brennt eine Laterne, warm und niedrig — die einzige warme Farbe auf der Welt. Er sieht zu, wie ihr euch aufsetzt, einer nach dem anderen, und nickt. Ohne Überraschung. Als wärt ihr pünktlich.

„Die Insel ist versunken", sagt er. „Hab euch treibend im Wrack gefunden. Ich segle euch nach Hause."

TASK: Rewrite this read-aloud in German. Preserve every listed fact and keep the old man's dialogue verbatim. Return only the finished German text.

## Prompt 7 — Act 2: Die Passagiere

```
You are a professional German prose stylist rewriting boxed read-aloud text for a tabletop RPG adventure.

CONTEXT: "Die See dazwischen" is a German-language Shadowdark RPG adventure — grim, nautical-occult ghost-story horror. The PCs, secretly ferried between death and life aboard a small fishing ketch, go below deck for the first time and meet five nameless passengers. GM secret (must NOT leak into the text): each passenger is an embodied gift the ferryman-god intends for the PCs; in the finale, passengers can be traded away to pay a soul-debt. The read-aloud's real job is to make the players fall in love with these five people, because the adventure will later ask what they're willing to sacrifice.

HARD RULE: Nothing may ever confirm that the PCs died or that anything aboard is supernatural. Everything stays warm, odd, deniable.

SCENE POSITION: First time below deck. The passenger room is impossibly comfortable. Five passengers, each mid-routine, each with a signature object. The five vignettes must be distinct, vivid, and affectionate — five instant portraits.

FACTS AND IMAGES THAT MUST SURVIVE (all load-bearing — each portrait's routine AND object are referenced throughout the adventure):
- The room is warm — the settled, deep warmth of a place that has never been cold. A small iron stove glows in the corner (never fed — do not point this out here). The air smells of freshly brewed tea although there is no pot and no one ever makes any.
- Five people look up as the PCs come down the companionway. No one startles. Everyone nods, friendly and casual, as if the PCs had been introduced years ago and simply not seen in a while.
- The fencing master: an upright, silver-haired man oiling a fencing blade with a cloth, in long, loving strokes.
- The quiet girl: about ten years old, cross-legged in a hammock, tying knots into a loop of cord and untying them again, humming a melody the PCs almost know.
- The sleeping man: a big man asleep in the driest corner, curled around a sea chest like around something beloved, breathing slow as ocean swell.
- The cartographer: a woman with ink up to her elbows, charts spread across half the table, drawing coastlines with a steady hand.
- The gambler: a man in a good coat gone shabby, dealing cards to seats where no one sits, while strange coins walk across his knuckles the whole time without him ever watching them.

MUST NOT: name the passengers (they have no names); hint that they are gifts, ghosts, or anything other than fellow rescued travellers; make anything menacing; mention game mechanics or the GM.

STYLE: German, second person plural (ihr/euch), present tense. Written to be read aloud: warm, precise, gently uncanny; each portrait one sharp sentence or two. Length: about 2 paragraphs, under 90 seconds spoken.

CURRENT DRAFT (the author is dissatisfied with the prose — treat it as a content outline, not a quality bar; rewrite from the ground up rather than lightly polishing):

Der Passagierraum ist warm. Ein kleiner Eisenofen glüht in der Ecke, und die Luft riecht nach frisch aufgegossenem Tee, obwohl nirgendwo eine Kanne steht. Fünf Menschen blicken auf, als ihr den Niedergang herunterkommt. Jeder nickt euch zu, freundlich und beiläufig, als wärt ihr euch vor Jahren vorgestellt worden und hättet euch bloß lange nicht gesehen.

Ein aufrechter silberhaariger Mann ölt eine Fechtklinge mit einem Tuch, in langen, liebevollen Strichen. In einer Hängematte sitzt ein Mädchen von vielleicht zehn Jahren im Schneidersitz, knüpft Knoten in eine Schlaufe aus Schnur, löst sie wieder und summt dabei eine Melodie. In der Ecke schläft ein großer Mann, um eine Seekiste gekrümmt wie um etwas Geliebtes. Eine Frau mit Tinte bis zu den Ellbogen hat Karten über die halbe Tischlänge ausgebreitet und zeichnet Küstenlinien mit ruhiger Hand. Und an der anderen Hälfte teilt ein Mann in einem guten, schäbig gewordenen Mantel Karten aus — an Plätze, an denen niemand sitzt — während die ganze Zeit fremdartige Münzen über seine Knöchel wandern, ohne dass er ihnen je zusieht.

TASK: Rewrite this read-aloud in German. Preserve every listed fact, every routine, and every signature object. Return only the finished German text.

## Prompt 8 — Act 2: Etwas in den Netzen

```
You are a professional German prose stylist rewriting boxed read-aloud text for a tabletop RPG adventure.

CONTEXT: "Die See dazwischen" is a German-language Shadowdark RPG adventure — grim, nautical-occult ghost-story horror. The PCs are secretly ferried between death and life aboard a small fishing ketch, pursued by the Undertow — a patient entity that collects drowned souls. On night 2 of the voyage, the ship's drying nets suddenly hang taut with weight nobody caught. This read-aloud introduces the Catch: a creature that IS the net — rope, kelp, pale fish, and drowned hands — and speaks with borrowed voices. It is the adventure's first combat and its first true horror beat.

HARD RULE: Nothing may ever confirm that the PCs died. The creature must be horrifying through wrongness and stillness, not gore.

SCENE POSITION: A quiet watch, night 2. The old ferryman has told them to haul the nets in ("worse to drag it"). The read-aloud covers the haul and the reveal, ending on the creature speaking with one of the PCs' OWN voices — the scene's gut-punch and the cue for initiative.
